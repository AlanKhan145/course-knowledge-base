# 55. Face Lighting Study

> **Module:** 07 Light Color & Perspective  
> **Level:** Intermediate  
> **Suggested practice:** 25–45 minutes  
> **Original source:** [Face Lighting Study](https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-shade-an-anime-face-in-different-lighting/) · AnimeOutline

## Learning objective

Separate form shadows from cast shadows on an anime portrait.

## Core principle

A strong drawing is easier to edit when large masses, directional guides, and relationships are established before any fine rendering. For **face lighting study**, treat each visible line as evidence of an underlying form or a deliberate graphic design choice. Begin lightly, compare against a reference, and separate the *rough construction* from the *refined final drawing*.

## Guided workshop

1. **Choose the key-light direction with an arrow.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.
2. **Mark face planes facing toward and away from the light.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.
3. **Place shadows from hair, nose, and chin.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.
4. **Keep the number of value groups small for cel shading.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.

## Practice assignment

**Main exercise:** Shade one face from front, side, and below.

- **Warm-up (5 min):** Draw several quick studies using only large shapes and gesture lines.
- **Construction (10–15 min):** Produce a rough version with visible guides and a consistent reference.
- **Refinement (10–15 min):** Correct the largest issues, remove unnecessary guides, and strengthen important contours.
- **Review (5 min):** Compare both stages, write down two improvements and one point to practice again.

## Common pitfall

**Watch out for:** Moving cast shadows without moving the light source.

Correct the issue in the rough phase rather than hiding it with extra contour lines or shading. A useful test is to shrink your drawing to thumbnail size; major shapes should remain intelligible.

# 54. One-Point Perspective Room

> **Module:** 07 Light Color & Perspective  
> **Level:** Intermediate  
> **Suggested practice:** 25–45 minutes

## Learning objective

Use convergence to build a simple believable room.

## Core principle

A strong drawing is easier to edit when large masses, directional guides, and relationships are established before any fine rendering. For **one-point perspective room**, treat each visible line as evidence of an underlying form or a deliberate graphic design choice. Begin lightly, compare against a reference, and separate the *rough construction* from the *refined final drawing*.

## Guided workshop

1. **Draw a horizon and mark a single vanishing point.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.
2. **Block back wall, floor, and ceiling.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.
3. **Connect furniture depth edges toward the vanishing point.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.
4. **Check that verticals and horizontals remain consistent.** After the step, compare the silhouette and relative placement to your reference.

## Practice assignment

**Main exercise:** Sketch a room with desk, bookshelf, and one window.

- **Warm-up (5 min):** Draw several quick studies using only large shapes and gesture lines.
- **Construction (10–15 min):** Produce a rough version with visible guides and a consistent reference.
- **Refinement (10–15 min):** Correct the largest issues, remove unnecessary guides, and strengthen important contours.
- **Review (5 min):** Compare both stages, write down two improvements and one point to practice again.

## Common pitfall

**Watch out for:** Sending parallel depth edges to different vanishing points.

Correct the issue in the rough phase rather than hiding it with extra contour lines or shading. A useful test is to shrink your drawing to thumbnail size; major shapes should remain intelligible.

TOEIC PART 7 · Bài 1 · Email: Friday Product Training

From: Laura Kim, Sales Training Manager
To: Sales Team
Subject: Updated plan for Friday product training

Thank you for registering for Friday's product training. There are two changes to the schedule that was sent on Monday. First, the session will take place in Room 402, not Room 204, because the smaller room is being used for a client presentation. Second, the training will begin at 9:30 a.m., thirty minutes later than originally planned. Please arrive by 9:15 so that we can check attendance and distribute name cards before the first presentation starts.

The morning will focus on the new X-Series products. During the first hour, Daniel Cho from Product Development will explain the main features and answer technical questions. After a short break at 10:45, we will practice explaining prices, warranties, and delivery options to customers. Please bring the printed price list that was placed in your mailbox yesterday. You may also bring a tablet for taking notes, but laptops are not required.

The final activity will be a role-play in pairs, and the session should finish at approximately 12:15 p.m. Employees who cannot attend must email me before Thursday at noon. I will arrange a make-up session next Tuesday from 3:00 to 5:00 p.m., but space is limited to eight people and your manager must approve the change.

Phần A · Multiple Choice

1. What is the main purpose of the email?

2. Why was the training moved to Room 402?

3. What will participants do after the 10:45 break?

4. What does the email say about laptops?

5. The phrase make-up session is closest in meaning to ________.

Phần B · True / False / Not Given

1. The training will start earlier than originally planned.

2. Employees who miss Friday can attend another session next Tuesday if approved.

3. The email states that lunch will be provided after the training.

TOEIC PART 7 · Bài 2 · Notice: Riverside Library Renovation

RIVERSIDE CITY LIBRARY — SERVICE NOTICE

Beginning Monday, November 2, the second floor of Riverside City Library will be closed for approximately three weeks while new lighting, electrical outlets, and study desks are installed. The work is part of a larger renovation project designed to create more quiet study space and improve access to power for visitors using laptops and tablets.

During the renovation, the first floor will remain open from 8:30 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. Monday through Friday and from 10:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. on weekends. The information desk, public computers, newspaper area, and children's collection will continue to operate normally. Most books from the second floor have been moved to temporary shelves near the west entrance. However, older magazines and several reference collections will be unavailable until the work is complete.

Study Room B will also be closed during the first week because workers need access to the electrical panel behind it. Customers who have already reserved the room will be contacted by library staff and offered another space when possible. Group Study Rooms A and C can still be booked online, although demand is expected to be high.

Visitors who need a completely quiet place are encouraged to use the Northside Branch, located 1.2 kilometers away on King Street. Library cards are valid at both locations, and borrowed materials may be returned to either branch.

Phần A · Multiple Choice

1. What is the main reason for the second-floor closure?

2. Which service will remain available on the first floor?

3. What will happen to people who reserved Study Room B?

4. Why does the notice mention the Northside Branch?

5. The word demand is closest in meaning to ________.

Phần B · True / False / Not Given

1. The first floor will close completely during the renovation.

2. Some materials will not be available until the work is finished.

3. The notice gives the exact date when the second floor will reopen.

IELTS READING · Passage 1 · Urban Green Spaces

As cities become denser, planners are paying closer attention to parks, tree-lined streets, community gardens and other forms of urban greenery. These spaces are often discussed as if they provide a single, universal benefit, but researchers increasingly argue that their effects depend on how they are designed, maintained and used.

One frequently reported advantage is that green areas create opportunities for physical activity. A well-connected park with safe walking paths may encourage residents to walk or exercise more often. Trees can also reduce surface temperatures by providing shade, which may be particularly useful during periods of extreme heat. However, simply adding plants does not automatically produce these outcomes. A small garden that is difficult to reach, poorly lit at night or considered unsafe may be used by very few people.

Researchers also study the relationship between green spaces and mental well-being. Some studies have found associations between regular contact with nature and lower levels of reported stress. Yet it is difficult to determine exactly why this happens. People who live near attractive parks may also have access to other advantages, such as quieter streets, better housing or more leisure time. For this reason, researchers must be careful not to assume that greenery alone causes every positive result.

Another issue is equality of access. Large investment in a new park can improve a neighborhood, but it may also increase property values and rents. If lower-income residents are forced to move away, the people who were supposed to benefit may not remain in the area long enough to enjoy the improvements. Some cities are therefore combining green projects with policies intended to protect affordable housing.

Overall, urban green spaces can support healthier and more comfortable cities, but their value depends on location, accessibility, safety, maintenance and the wider social conditions of the neighborhood.

Phần A · Paraphrase

1. Which statement best paraphrases the first paragraph?

2. Why might a park fail to increase physical activity?

3. What problem do researchers face when studying mental well-being?

Phần B · True / False / Not Given

1. Trees can help reduce surface temperatures by providing shade.

2. The passage claims that greenery is the only cause of lower stress.

3. The passage gives the exact percentage by which rents rise after a new park is built.

4. Some cities combine green projects with affordable-housing policies.

Phần C · Reading comprehension

1. What is the main purpose of the passage?

2. What can happen when a new park increases local property values?

3. Which factor is NOT listed in the conclusion as affecting the value of green spaces?

IELTS READING · Passage 2 · Community Libraries in a Digital Age

For many years, public libraries were mainly associated with shelves of printed books. Today, however, community libraries in many towns are expanding their role. In addition to lending books, some provide computer access, language clubs, homework support, job-search workshops and rooms where local groups can meet.

This change is partly a response to unequal access to digital technology. Although smartphones are common, not everyone has a reliable computer, a fast internet connection or a quiet place to complete online forms and assignments. A library can therefore function as a practical workspace as well as a source of information. For job seekers, access to a computer and a staff member who can explain how to upload a document may be more immediately useful than borrowing a book.

The broader role of libraries creates challenges. Traditional users may expect silence, while other visitors need to talk during language practice or group projects. Some buildings solve this conflict by separating silent zones from collaborative rooms. Others schedule noisier activities at particular times. The success of these solutions depends heavily on the size and design of the building.

Funding is another concern. New services require equipment, staff time and maintenance. A town may be able to buy computers through a one-time grant, but replacing broken devices several years later requires a continuing budget. For this reason, managers often introduce services gradually and collect information about which activities are most heavily used.

Supporters of the modern community-library model argue that the institution is not abandoning books. Instead, it is extending the original idea of shared access to knowledge. Critics, however, worry that libraries may attempt to provide too many services with limited staff. The debate is therefore less about whether libraries should change at all and more about how they can balance different needs without losing their core purpose.

IELTS READING · Passage 3 · Public Transport and Commuting

Rapidly growing cities often face the same problem: as more people travel to work, school and services, roads become crowded and journey times become less predictable. Public transport is frequently presented as a solution, but adding more buses or trains is only one part of the challenge.

Passengers generally care about several features at the same time. Frequency matters because long gaps between services make spontaneous travel difficult. Reliability is equally important. A bus that is scheduled every ten minutes but regularly arrives twenty minutes late may be less useful than a slightly less frequent service that follows its timetable. Connections between routes also matter, especially for passengers who must transfer from a bus to a train or from one bus line to another.

Information can influence how people experience the same waiting time. Real-time displays and mobile applications can show when the next vehicle is expected. These systems do not make a delayed bus arrive faster, but they reduce uncertainty. A passenger who knows that the next bus will arrive in eighteen minutes may decide to walk to another stop, buy a drink or take a different route instead of waiting without information.

Price is another factor, although cheap fares alone do not guarantee high use. If a service is infrequent, uncomfortable or difficult to reach, passengers with other options may still choose private cars. On the other hand, very high fares can make public transport unaffordable for lower-income residents, even when the service is reliable.

Some transport authorities are therefore focusing on the complete journey rather than a single vehicle. This includes safe walking routes to stations, clear signs, simple ticket systems and coordinated timetables. The goal is to make public transport not only available but also convenient enough to compete with private travel for a wide range of daily trips.

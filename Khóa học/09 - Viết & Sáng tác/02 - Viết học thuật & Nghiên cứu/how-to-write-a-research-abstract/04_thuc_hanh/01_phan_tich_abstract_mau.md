# 14 — Phân tích một Abstract mẫu

## Abstract minh họa

> Urban expansion increases contact between human settlements and wildlife habitats, raising concern about zoonotic transmission. This study investigated whether proximity to squirrel habitats was associated with reported rabies cases in domestic animals. We conducted a cross-sectional analysis of surveillance records collected from 2007–2015 and evaluated associations between urban proximity and rabies incidence. Areas closer to squirrel habitats showed higher reported rabies incidence than more distant areas. These findings suggest that wildlife–urban interfaces may be useful targets for surveillance, although longitudinal studies are needed to evaluate causality.
